## 5. Learning Journey
 
This course gave me practical exposure to the cyber world, and two pieces of work stood out.
 
**Assignment 2.** I do not have much internship experience in cybersecurity, so Assignment 2 gave me a broader view of how the field works in practice. It made the topics from lectures feel less abstract, and it showed me how they connect to real security tasks. It also helped me see which areas I want to study in more depth, which matters to me because I plan to move into research.
 
**Lab 9.2.6** The lab activity use the CyberOps virtual machine, and they were the first time I used Wireshark from the command line. Before this I had only used its graphical interface. Working from the terminal helped me understand what Wireshark is actually doing when it captures, filters and reads packets, instead of just clicking through windows. It also connected to my earlier firewall project, where I worked with TCP, UDP and ICMP packets in Python.
 
**Something that went wrong.** I could not get the CyberOps VM running at first. There was almost no documentation on how to install it on a Mac with Apple silicon in the cisco web, so the standard instructions did not work for my setup. I diagnosed the problem by working out that the missing piece was guidance for my specific environment, not a mistake in the steps I was following. I fixed it by searching for external documentation, including a Reddit discussion from people who had faced the same issue, and then working through the installation with Claude until the VM finally ran. The lesson I took away is that when official documentation is missing, I should look for community sources, compare them carefully, and test one change at a time.
 
**What I still find challenging.** Reading and understanding an actual network topology is still quite difficult for me. I also still forget the commands for configuring routers and switches. I can follow a guided lab, but I cannot yet set up a topology confidently from memory.
 
**My improvement goal.** I will practise network topologies and router and switch configuration regularly, and I aim to be comfortable with them before the mid-semester break begins. I will measure this by building three different topologies in Cisco Packet Tracer, configuring every router and switch from the command line without looking at notes, and confirming that the devices can reach each other with ping tests. I will keep a practice log of each attempt and the commands I forgot, and I will repeat the commands I missed until I can finish a topology without any mistakes.
